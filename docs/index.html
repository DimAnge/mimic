Mimic is a Raspberry Pi Pico W, a 128×64 OLED and two buttons. It keeps an eye on the weather, your calendar, your music and your machine — and pulls a face about all of it.

Eight screens, two buttons

tap a row to send it to the screen

One button walks the tabs and goes home when you hold it. The other does whatever the current screen needs. Nothing is more than two presses away.

What it is made of

about £25 of parts

Everything sits on a half-size breadboard inside a printed case. No soldering — female-to-male jumpers, and buttons that come pre-assembled on discs.

Build one

an evening, plus print time

Steps one to five get a face on the screen. Six and seven are what make it useful.

  1. Flash CircuitPython

    Hold BOOTSEL, plug the Pico W in, drop the .uf2 on the RPI-RP2 drive. It reboots as a drive called CIRCUITPY.

  2. Copy the libraries

    From the CircuitPython library bundle, copy the display, text, connection-manager and requests modules into /lib on CIRCUITPY.

  3. Wire it up

    Nine jumpers, following the map above. Check every connection before you trust anything else, and check it again after any change to the case.

  4. Add your settings

    Put your Wi-Fi details and the address of your PC in settings.toml. Remember the radio is 2.4 GHz only.

    CIRCUITPY_WIFI_SSID = "your-network"
CIRCUITPY_WIFI_PASSWORD = "your-password"
BRIDGE_HOST = "192.168.1.42"
CITY = "Athens"

  5. Deploy

    Copy the six board files across and flush the write. Skipping sync is how you get a half-written file and a board that boots to an error.

    cp code.py eyes.py dino.py eightball.py sfx.py icons.py \
   /media/$USER/CIRCUITPY/ && sync
tio /dev/ttyACM0   # watch it boot

  6. Start the bridge

    Run bridge.py on the machine Mimic sits next to, then install it as a user service so it comes back after a reboot.

    systemctl --user enable --now mimic-bridge

  7. Print the case

    Three parts from one OpenSCAD file: the tray, a lid with a cable slot, and a spacer that sets the stack height. The screen stand and the button discs come from MakerWorld.

The bridge

bridge.py, standard library only

A Pico W has no business holding your Spotify tokens or your calendar URL. So it doesn't. A small HTTP server on your PC does the talking and hands Mimic plain numbers over your own network.

GET /stats

Your machine, in four numbers

CPU load, memory, uptime and disk, read on the PC where that information already lives.

GET /next

The meeting popup

The bridge parses your calendar's private iCal feed and returns only the next event and its start time.

GET /now

Music and usage

Spotify playback state and how much of your Claude usage window is gone. Tokens are refreshed on the PC and never leave it.

The case

OpenSCAD, v6

Three custom parts printed from one OpenSCAD file, plus a screen stand and button assemblies from MakerWorld. The breadboard drops into the tray, the spacer sets the stack height, the lid lifts off.

tray.stl

The body

Breadboard pocket, USB opening on the right wall, and counterbores for the pre-assembled button discs.

lid.stl

The lid

Locating lip and a cable pass-through. Lifts off whenever you want to get at the breadboard.

spacer.stl

The spacer

Sets the height of the stack inside the tray so the board and breadboard sit where the openings expect them.
